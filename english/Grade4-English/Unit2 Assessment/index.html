The Cracked Jug

1

Once there lived a young woman who traveled on foot to a water well. Carrying two jugs, which looked like pots, she walked along the same road every day. Her two jugs looked very similar, but one had an unfortunate crack from its middle to its base.

2

One evening, the jug without a crack boasted rather cruelly to the cracked jug, "Our owner prefers me over you, you know. I am perfect, and not flawed and cracked like you."

3

"Flawed?" questioned the cracked jug. "Whatever do you mean? I don't believe I've done anything wrong to displease our lovely owner."

4

The jug without a crack sneered. "Foolish jug! You are cracked! Water drips out of you, and by the time we arrive home, your contents are gone! There's not a drop of water left in you for our owner to wash or cook with or to drink."

5

The cracked jug felt powerfully wounded by these nasty comments. He had always been so proud to serve their owner, but he now realized that all this time, all these years, he had failed her — he'd been disappointing her all along.

6

The next day, the cracked jug struggled bravely to hold in all the water, but, alas, he could not. The water still dripped, dripped, dripped out. Feeling shame, the jug mumbled to his owner, "I am so sorry for letting you down."

7

The young woman just smiled gratefully at the cracked jug as she pointed to the sides of the road. She said, "Dearest jug, do you see all of the wonderful flowers along the road? Do you know that I have been scattering and planting the seeds for those flowers?"

8

"And every day as I walk from the entrance of the walkway to this very spot, I water the blossoming flowers with you, my dear cracked jug! Everyone on this road enjoys the flowers that you water. Do you not see that, without you, there would be no beautiful flowers at all!"

The two jugs regarded each other in a new light. They realized that they were both perfect in their own way.

1In Panel 2, what does the boasting by the jug without a crack reveal about him?

2In Panel 4, what character trait does the jug without a crack show through his words and actions?

3What does the jug without a crack mean when he says to the other jug, "You are cracked!"?

4In Panel 5, what does the word wounded show about the cracked jug?

5Which word from Panels 5 and 6 shows that the cracked jug wants to please his owner?

6Why did the woman smile at the cracked jug after the jug said he was sorry?

7How does the woman show that she is a caring person?

8What do the jugs understand at the end of the story?

9What does a recording of the story help listeners understand better?
